> Thank you for this constructive suggestion. The pristine graphite used in our study had a water contact angle of 59.0°, and we therefore regard it as a hydrophilic surface. We cannot rule out the possibility that tuning such factors could enable water slip even on hydrophobic surfaces. Therefore, to emphasize our key finding that wettability itself does not affect the water slip length, as predicted by molecular dynamics simulations, and to better reflect the long-standing controversy regarding slip length on hydrophobic surfaces, we have revised the title to “Hydrophobicity Does Not Affect Water Slip Length: Insights from Slip Length Mapping.” We believe that the revised title is more accurate and appropriate.
